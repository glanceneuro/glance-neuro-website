What it is

GLANCE records from Intan RHD2000-style neural chips on a MicroZed (Xilinx Zynq-7020) with a custom carrier board. The programmable logic talks to up to two cables of amplifiers over a DDR SPI protocol; the processor streams the data to your host over gigabit Ethernet.

It is built for experiments that have to react. Every 30 kHz sample leaves the board as its own UDP datagram — never batched, never held for a bigger packet — so nothing on the acquisition side stands between a spike and your detector. Per-stream sequence numbers make packet loss provable rather than suspected.

The firmware, the FPGA design, the carrier PCB and the Open Ephys plugin are all open, and all developed in the open.

On the board

stream 2

LFP extraction in hardware

A two-stage FIR cascade decimates the 30 kHz broadband to a ~3 kHz local field potential band inside the FPGA, arriving as a second stream on the same port — no host-side filtering, no extra cable.

stream 3

Headstage motion

A BNO055 IMU on each cable rides the freed second data pair over I²C, giving fused orientation, acceleration and rotation stamped with the same master clock as the neural samples — so motion and spikes align with no host-side clock matching.

analog out

Stimulus playback

Arbitrary waveforms out of two on-board SMA connectors via a dual-channel DAC70502, software- or hardware-triggered, looping or one-shot, and driven by the FPGA itself so playback never depends on the network.

runtime

Reconfigurable fabrics

The board carries several FPGA configurations on its SD card and swaps between them on command, trading a cable's second data pair for the IMU bus when a headstage needs it. It works out what is plugged in and loads the one that fits.
